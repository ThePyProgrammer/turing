# Research Plan: Philosophical Foundations of Autonomous ML Research

## Questions
1. **Taste vs execution divide:** Who beyond Amy Tam has written about the distinction between problem selection (taste) and mechanical execution in research? What do Popper, Kuhn, Lakatos, and contemporary AI researchers say?
2. **Self-evaluation epistemology:** What is the philosophical basis for separating hypothesis generation from evaluation? How does Goodhart's Law apply to ML agents? What does the observer effect mean for automated science?
3. **Human-AI research collaboration:** What do leading researchers (Hassabis, LeCun, Nielsen, etc.) say about human judgment in automated experimentation? What are the contemporary 2024-2026 perspectives?
4. **Automated science precedents:** What historical and contemporary examples exist of automated scientific discovery? (Robot Scientist, AlphaFold, self-driving labs)

## Strategy
- 3 parallel researcher subagents covering disjoint dimensions:
  - R1: Philosophy of science + taste/judgment (Popper, Kuhn, Lakatos, Polányi, contemporary philosophy)
  - R2: Goodhart's Law, self-evaluation, automated science, observer effect, AI safety applied to research
  - R3: Contemporary voices 2024-2026 (Hassabis, LeCun, Nielsen, Karpathy, autoresearch discourse, self-driving labs)
- Expected: 1-2 rounds

## Acceptance Criteria
- [ ] All 4 key questions answered with ≥2 independent sources
- [ ] Contradictions identified and addressed
- [ ] No single-source claims on critical findings
- [ ] At least 3 contemporary (2024-2026) sources beyond Amy Tam
- [ ] At least 2 historical philosophy of science references
